    Extracurricular Activities

    Build skills outside the classroom.

    This section covers activities that help students grow through leadership, research, debate, and real-world communication.

    Model United Nations

    Model United Nations (MUN) is an academic simulation where students represent countries, debate global problems, and write diplomatic documents. It combines public speaking, research, negotiation, teamwork, and formal writing in one activity.

    What you do in MUN

    • Represent a country or a delegation.
    • Research a topic from that country's perspective.
    • Write a position paper before the conference.
    • Debate, negotiate, and form alliances during committee.
    • Write and support resolutions that propose solutions.

    Why it helps self-development

    • Improves confidence in public speaking.
    • Builds research and critical thinking skills.
    • Teaches diplomatic language and formal communication.
    • Develops leadership, teamwork, and negotiation.
    • Helps students understand international relations.

    Basic MUN procedure

    In a typical committee, delegates present their country's stance through speeches. They then debate, ask questions, negotiate in moderated and unmoderated caucuses, and write draft resolutions. At the end, delegates vote on the resolutions. The goal is not only to enhance public speaking skills, but also to cooperate and create realistic solutions.

    Position paper example

    Committee: Social, Humanitarian and Cultural Committee (SOCHUM)
Country: Arab Republic of Egypt
Agenda: Safeguarding Cultural Heritage in Conflict Zones

Safeguarding Cultural Heritage in Conflict Zones

In a period when radical militaristic actions are still being carried out, protecting a
nation's patrimonium may require significant effort, which can sometimes be unfeasible
if undertaken by a single state. Cultural heritage represents a pillar of a country's
history, serving as direct proof of the past and of the enigmas it has left to be deciphered.

The Arab Republic of Egypt is deeply aware of the importance of safeguarding its cultural
heritage despite the obstacles imposed by conflict and instability. Measures have been taken
to protect cultural heritage in several fields, including, but not limited to, the social and
legal spheres. Nevertheless, the challenges remain particularly difficult when cultural
genocide occurs. Cultural diplomacy is therefore of utmost importance in preventing unwanted
events, such as the looting that took place in Cairo in 2011.

Among the many examples of efforts to address this issue, Egypt has adopted various laws,
including Law No. 117 of 1983 and Law No. 144 of 2006, both of which support the preservation
of cultural heritage. Conflict zones make the situation even more challenging, especially when
terrorist organizations are also involved.

Thus, the Arab Republic of Egypt considers that one of the most effective ways to address the
obstacles threatening cultural heritage is to collaborate with INTERPOL, strengthen
international cooperation and promote the digitalization of endangered cultural elements.

    Resolution example

    FORUM: Third Committee
QUESTION OF: Safeguarding Cultural Heritage in Conflict Zones
SPONSOR: United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland
CO-SPONSORS: Canada, Arab Republic of Egypt, French Republic, Republic of India,
State of Israel, United Mexican States, Kingdom of the Netherlands, Kingdom of Sweden,
Republic of Türkiye, Ukraine, United Republic of Tanzania, United States of America,
Human Rights Watch

THE THIRD COMMITTEE,

Fully aware of the 1954 Hague Convention for the Protection of Cultural Property in
the Event of Armed Conflict,

Taking into account the UNESCO convention concerning the intentional destruction of
cultural heritage sites,

Keeping in mind the Hague Convention of 1899 and 1907,

Recognizing the threats imposed by globalization upon all forms of cultural heritage,

Conscious of accidental destruction of cultural heritage through military operations,
especially clandestine military operations,

1. Encourages the creation of specialized groups, in collaboration with INTERPOL, that
   would consist of trained experts who would act to protect vulnerable cultural sites:
   a) facilitating safe transportation of artefacts, considering:
      i. the area of the armed conflict,
      ii. the infrastructure,
   b) protecting heritage from terrorist groups,
   c) holding periodic training sessions, conducted quarterly;

2. Strongly emphasizes the importance of taking legal actions against illicit trade of
   artefacts with cultural importance, by:
   a) increasing monitoring of international export and trade at the borders,
   b) implementing fines for any kind of illegal possession of cultural heritage;

3. Draws attention to the creation of awareness campaigns focused on engaging communities:
   a) creating online campaigns,
   b) creating projects in schools,
   c) organising trips to war-free cultural sites,
   d) spreading informational materials;

4. Further recommends strengthening international cooperation between Member States,
   cultural institutions and relevant organisations in order to improve the protection,
   identification and recovery of endangered cultural heritage in conflict zones.

    A resolution represents the committee's proposed solution to the topic. It helps delegates practise problem-solving, legal-style writing, teamwork, and persuasive negotiation, because the document must convince others to support it.
